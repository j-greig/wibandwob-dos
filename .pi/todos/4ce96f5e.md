## Brief

The v2 ambient-presence piece has the right CONCEPT (spoken word, key change, 
additive structure) but the musical execution is too primitive. The harmony 
is static within sections, the arp is mechanical, the voices don't interact.

## Feedback from human

- Think more like Brian Eno or Nils Frahm
- Study how their music works and evolves over a piece
- Interplay between bass, mid, and high registers
- Pacing, rhythm, emotion — not just "place sounds at timestamps"
- Research those composers. Understand WHY their music moves people.
- Write actual musical score notes before coding
- Wib (artist) and Wob (scientist) should collaborate on this properly
- Take breaks. Let ideas develop. Eureka moments happen when you relax.
- This should be a PROCESS that develops over time, not a one-shot script
- When done: do an unexpected remix in a totally different genre

## Process

1. RESEARCH: Study Eno (Music for Airports, Ambient 1, Discreet Music) and 
   Frahm (Says, All Melody, Felt). Write notes on what makes them work 
   musically. Not vibes — actual compositional technique.

2. SCORE: Write a musical score document in scratch/compositions/ before 
   touching code. Notes, intervals, voicing decisions, arc, emotional intent 
   per section. Think on paper first.

3. WALK: Metaphorical. Step away from the code. Let the score sit. Come back 
   with fresh ears. Revise the score before implementing.

4. COMPOSE v3: Implement with the chiptune-bricks toolkit. Focus on:
   - Harmonic movement (not just key changes — voice leading, suspensions,
     modal interchange, pedal tones)
   - Rhythmic evolution (not metronomic arp — phase relationships, 
     polyrhythm, deliberate drift)
   - Register interplay (bass responds to melody, melody responds to texture)
   - Dynamic arc (not just volume — timbral evolution, density shifts)
   - Silence and space as compositional elements

5. REMIX: Take v3 and do something completely unexpected with it. Different 
   genre, different energy, different emotional register. A genuine creative 
   left turn.
